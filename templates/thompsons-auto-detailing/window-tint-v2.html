Common questions

What buyers ask us.

Ceramic or carbon — which should I get?+

Ceramic is the premium choice: the most heat and infrared rejection, the clearest optics, and the longest life — backed for life. Carbon is a great value with solid heat and glare reduction and the same 99% UV block. If your car bakes in the sun or you want the best, go ceramic; if budget is the priority, carbon still performs well.

Will tint actually keep my car cooler?+

Yes — especially ceramic. It rejects a large share of solar heat and infrared, so your cabin heats up far less and your AC doesn't have to work as hard. Carbon helps too, just not as much as ceramic.

Is window tint legal in Washington?+

Washington limits how dark your front side windows can be, while rear and back windows can usually go darker. We'll walk you through a shade that keeps you compliant for your vehicle — no guessing.

Does tint block UV rays?+

Both our carbon and ceramic films block 99% of UV rays. That protects your skin on long drives and keeps your seats, dash, and trim from fading and cracking over time.

How soon can I roll my windows down?+

Give the film 3–5 days to cure before rolling the windows down. A little haze or a few water pockets during the first few days is normal — it clears up as the tint dries. We'll send you home with a simple care sheet.

Will it bubble or turn purple?+

Not with our films. Bubbling and that purple fade come from cheap dyed tint. Carbon and ceramic stay true to color, and ceramic is backed for life against bubbling, peeling, and fading.

How long does the install take?+

Most vehicles are done in a few hours — usually same day. We'll give you an exact time when we quote your car.
